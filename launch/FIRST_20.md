# First 20 people

Aim: get 15–20 parents (or people who help them) to try Advocate in the first week, and learn what is confusing.
Send these one to one, not in a group. Give each person their own free access code if you like (see the bottom).

## Who to ask
- Parents of children with SEND you already know
- A SENDIASS adviser or charity worker you have met
- A teacher, SENCO or therapist who talks to parents about EHCPs
- Anyone who has been through an EHCP and would tell you honestly what is missing

## The message (text, WhatsApp or email)
> Hi [name], I've built something for parents going through an EHCP and I'd love your honest opinion.
> It's called Advocate. It tracks the legal deadlines (the 6, 16 and 20 week ones), reminds you before each one, has template letters, and prints a case pack. Your child's details stay on your own phone.
> It's free to try for 3 days, then £3.99 a month if you keep it. I've put a free access code below so you can try everything properly without paying.
> https://advocate.includeme.uk
> Code: [ADV-XXXX-XXXX] (tap "I have an access code" after you sign in)
> Could you have a play for 10 minutes and tell me anything that confused you, or anything you expected to see that isn't there? A reply to this message is perfect.

## Shorter version for a professional (SENDIASS, charity, SENCO)
> Hi [name], I've made a low-cost organiser for parents going through an EHCP: https://advocate.includeme.uk. It tracks the statutory deadlines, sends reminders and has template letters. Your free access code is [ADV-XXXX-XXXX]. I'd really value your view on whether it would help the parents you work with, and anything that is wrong or missing. It is not legal advice and says so.

## What to ask, after they have tried it (five questions)
1. What did you think the app was for, in your own words, in the first minute?
2. Was there anywhere you got stuck or did not know what to tap?
3. Which part would you actually use: the deadlines, reminders, letters or the case pack?
4. Is £3.99 a month about right, too much, or too little for what it does?
5. Would you tell another parent about it? What would you say?

## What to do with the answers
- Write each reply in one line in a list. When three people say the same thing, fix it.
- Ask permission before quoting anyone ("Can I use that on the website, with just your first name?").
- Remind yourself it is not a test of you. People being confused is the useful part.

## Making free access codes
Ask Claude: "make 10 free access codes for 30 days". Each code can be used once by one person by default. Options: days of access (1–366), how many people can use one code, when the code stops working (default 90 days), and a note so you remember who it is for (for example "Sam, SENDIASS"). Codes give full access with no card, and end on their own. They cannot be turned into money, and the person can subscribe any time to carry on.
